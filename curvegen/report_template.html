Rev F taperability & sculptability curves

Sensitivity cost (η, the image rms relative to natural weighting) versus achieved resolution at the 30 GHz reference frequency, measured from noise simulations of each Rev F subarray (4 h track, dec +24°) over a grid of Briggs robust and uv-taper values. The imaging curves constrain the PSF skirt — the azimuthally averaged PSF level at a radius of one FWHM (memo 65 metric 2b) — to a selectable threshold s0 (10% by default, the memo 121 criterion): at each resolution the cheapest (min-η) beam meeting the criterion is shown, and where no beam can meet it the best achievable skirt is reported and flagged (hollow), never trading resolution away.

    Imaging curves (cheapest beam meeting ≤ s0, else best achievable)

    At each target resolution the curve shows the minimum-η beam whose PSF skirt ≤ s0, computed by the shared interpolation engine (continuous in resolution). Angular resolution is the primary requirement: where no beam reaches s0, the criterion is relaxed to the best achievable skirt and hollow triangles mark those points (they do NOT meet the criterion; hover for the achieved skirt). Dashed verticals mark each subarray’s LAS at 30 GHz; the core-bearing subarrays share bmin and hence a common LAS. This is the operational look-up view for imaging projects (the memo 76 figure 1 analog).

    Point-source curves (skirt unconstrained)

    Same envelope over all beams regardless of PSF quality — applicable to point-source detection projects. Near-natural weighting the two curves differ most: a messy skirt is free here but costly to sculpt away.

    Why composite subarrays disagree with the calculator’s native beams

    The natural-weight PSF of core+spiral is a two-component composite: a narrow spiral-scale spike on a broad core-scale plateau. A single-Gaussian fit is ill-defined on such a beam — CASA’s fit lands at 286 mas here, while the memo-121-era value (147.7 mas) tracks the narrow spike. Neither is wrong; the “resolution” of an unsculpted composite beam is simply not a single number. The sculpted (imaging-curve) beams are quasi-Gaussian and do not suffer this ambiguity — one more reason the imaging curves are the operational reference.

    Caveats & status

    • core+mid is omitted from this report — its two-component beam needs sculpt targets anchored to the higher-resolution component (the v1 sweep found false minima there). The color slot is reserved; curves can drop in without re-mapping the others.
    • core-inner is memo 121’s CoreInner (57 antennas), taken here as the innermost 57 core antennas by radius from the core centroid — an assumed membership rule, to be verified against Viviana’s list.
    • LAS = 0.80 λ/bmin uniformly for the 18-m subarrays (memo-67 core value applied across the set); per-subarray disk-simulation measurements eventually.
    • η is assumed frequency-invariant when the curves are scaled from 30 GHz (PLAN.md open question 2); continuum fractional-bandwidth effects are not yet modeled.
    • Curves assume the memo-121 fixed-FOV single-pointing convention.
